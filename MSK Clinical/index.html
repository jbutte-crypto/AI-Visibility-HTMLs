01 · The behavior shift that’s already happening

People are searching and asking questions differently. And getting answers before clicking on anything.

For decades, search worked the same way: someone typed a question into Google, got a list of links, and clicked the one that looked most helpful. That model is breaking down. AI tools like Google's own AI summaries, ChatGPT, and Perplexity are now answering questions directly, right at the top of the page or without Google involved at all. The person asking may never click a single link.

The bottom line

MSK’s organic traffic and referral network won’t vanish overnight. But the share of patients choosing a provider, a specialty, or a hospital based on an AI-generated answer, rather than a Google search or a referral, is growing every month. Getting ahead of this now is meaningfully easier than trying to catch up once competing systems are already the ones AI cites.

02 · The mechanics in plain terms

The old way and the new way.

Understanding this doesn't require a technical background. The change is actually pretty simple: the path from question to answer has gotten shorter, and a middleman now decides who gets credit.

Before AI Search
How people found you
1
Person asks a question. Typed into Google or another search engine.
2
A list of links appears: your site, among your competitors and editorial sites. Whoever ranked highest got the most clicks.
3
Person clicks your site, reads it, explores it, and becomes a patient or comes back later.
Good SEO = high ranking = more clicks to your site.
With AI Search
How people find you now
1
Person asks a question, same as before, but now also via ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google's AI Overview feature.
2
The AI answers directly. It reads dozens of websites, synthesizes an answer, and cites 2–3 sources. Most people read the AI's answer and stop there.
3
The cited sources get the clicks. Only the sites that AI chose to reference get visited. If you’re not one of them, you’re invisible in that answer.
AI Visibility = being the brand the AI cites when it answers your audience's questions.
03 · For MSK specifically

Patients are already asking AI where to go for cancer care.

People searching for a doctor, a specialist, or the right hospital have always asked questions. The difference now is where they’re asking them, and whether MSK shows up in the answer, next to MD Anderson Cancer Center, City of Hope, and Mayo Clinic.

The next front door to healthcare won’t always be your homepage. Increasingly, it’ll be an answer generated somewhere else.

Where's the best place to get a second opinion on a cancer diagnosis near New York City?
Someone asks ChatGPT or Perplexity, right now, today
What the AI does
Synthesizes an answer from dozens of sources. Names specific health systems, practices, or providers. Cites 2 to 3 websites as references. The person reads the answer and trusts it or clicks straight through, without ever seeing a full list of New York City-area options.
The opportunity for MSK
If MSK’s site and provider profiles have the right content, structure, and signals in place, the AI cites mskcc.org as a recommended, trusted source, alongside or instead of a competing cancer center. That’s a referral that didn’t depend on ranking #1 on Google or a word-of-mouth network.
The SEO connection

The technical SEO groundwork Phase2 helped build with MSK is still the foundation AI tools would draw from today. This isn’t a replacement for that work; it extends it. The same content, authority signals, and technical structure that support search rankings are what AI tools use to decide who to cite. Reconnecting here isn’t starting over, it’s building on what’s already there.

04 · How this is different from regular SEO

SEO and AI Visibility work together. But they're not the same thing.

Traditional SEO is about getting your pages ranked highly in a list of links. AI Visibility is about making sure your brand and content are understood and trusted by the AI systems that synthesize answers. Most of the underlying work overlaps, but AI requires its own ongoing focus.

Content that AI can understand
AI tools scan health system sites looking for clear, authoritative answers: conditions treated, locations, insurance accepted, provider credentials. Content needs to be structured and written so it's easy for AI to pull from, not just easy for patients to read.
Technical signals under the hood
There are specific technical markers (called structured data) that tell AI tools what MSK is about, who its providers are, and why the organization is credible. This isn’t visible to patients, but matters a lot to AI systems.
Monitoring and ongoing adjustments
AI tools change constantly: which ones patients use, what they're asking about care, and whether MSK shows up in the answers instead of a competing cancer center. This requires ongoing monitoring, not a one-time setup.
What we're seeing across healthcare

One of the clearest lessons emerging across healthcare is that organizations don’t need to abandon their SEO strategy, they need to evolve it. The strongest performers are building on the technical and content foundations they already have while adapting them for how AI systems discover, interpret, and recommend information.

That’s why MSK came to mind.

05 · What organizations are doing today

What paying attention looks like right now.

For years, marketing teams have optimized for Google rankings. Increasingly, they're also optimizing for the questions AI answers before someone ever reaches Google. It's a subtle shift, but an important one, and organizations that start paying attention now will be much better positioned as AI becomes a bigger part of the patient journey.

The ongoing discipline

Understanding, improving, adapting

Much of this work isn't visible to patients. It's the ongoing effort of understanding how AI systems interpret an organization, and making incremental improvements over time.

Understanding
How AI platforms currently describe MSK, and where peer cancer centers are being recommended instead.
Improving
Making it easier for AI systems to understand provider expertise, service lines, locations, and trusted clinical content.
Adapting
Continuously responding as patient questions evolve and AI platforms change how they generate recommendations.
What success starts to look like
More physicians appearing in AI-generated recommendations
Service lines cited alongside the region's leading health systems
Content investments informed by real patient questions, not assumptions
Greater confidence that MSK is visible wherever patients begin their cancer care journey
The pattern worth noticing

Organizations that are getting ahead aren't treating this as a one-time optimization project. They're building an ongoing understanding of how AI platforms represent their organization, then making steady improvements as those platforms evolve.
